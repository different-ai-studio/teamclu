Shared skills and group chat for your team's AI agents.

TeamClu is a workspace where your team and its AI agents work in the same group chats. Publish a skill once and every teammate's agent follows it — without anyone re-teaching a private bot.

Agents run on your own machines

The agent is personal. That's the wrong shape for a team.

I teach my agent how we run a release. A teammate teaches theirs the same thing a month later, slightly differently. And the work itself happens in a chat window nobody else can see.

So TeamClu makes two things shared instead of private: what the agents know how to do, and the conversation they do it in.

Skills your whole team shares

A skill is a versioned team asset, not a snippet of prompt text that dies in someone's dotfiles.

Publish a skill to your team's registry with structured metadata and a changelog. Every teammate's agent follows the new version automatically — a background reconcile loop picks it up, and MQTT makes it near-instant. Nobody has to send the "please update your skill" message ever again.

Edit a shared skill locally and you get a conflict to resolve, never a silent overwrite. Publish a bad version and one click re-publishes the previous one as the new latest.

Sessions are group chats

Not one-to-one conversations with a bot. Teammates and agents share one thread, one timeline, one context.

Add teammates, @mention people, see who is online, and branch any agent reply into its own thread. The agent is a participant, not a service: it can be offline, permission-limited, or switched to a different model mid-conversation.

An agent that hasn't been mentioned doesn't interject. That is the design, not a fault.

Questions worth asking up front

Including the parts that aren't built yet.

Does the whole team share one context?
No. Skills, knowledge, roles and MCP config are team assets. Each member keeps a private context. That split is deliberate: it's what lets you share capability without sharing everything.
Can I keep a skill secret from some teammates?
Not today. Encryption is one key per team, shared by all members — it protects against the storage provider, not against colleagues. Per-person skill visibility isn't built yet.
Does an @mention grant access to a session?
No. In a channel, @bot means "this message is for the agent". It does not pull a person into a session.
Where do the agents actually run?
On your own machines, hosted by a local amuxd daemon — not in our cloud. You can reach them from WeCom, Feishu, Discord, KOOK, WeChat or Email, and the sessions stay the same.
What if someone publishes a bad version of a skill?
Every version is immutable and carries a changelog, and one click re-publishes an older version as the new latest. Your local edits are never silently overwritten by an update.
Is it production-ready?
It's v0.4.1 beta. Try it, file issues, expect rough edges. macOS builds are unsigned, so Gatekeeper will ask — the README covers the xattr -cr step.
